# S14 — Trommelen & van Loon (2016)

**Category:** [[D-Nutrition/index|D — Nutrition]]
**Type:** Scholarly

## Citation

Trommelen, J., & van Loon, L. J. C. (2016). Pre-sleep protein ingestion to improve the skeletal muscle adaptive response to exercise training. *Nutrients, 8*(12), 763. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu8120763

**Link:** https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/8/12/763

## Annotation

This review shows pre-sleep protein is digested overnight, raises overnight muscle protein synthesis, and can support strength and mass gains when paired with resistance training. It covers the sleep window as part of fueling — lifting, nutrition, and overnight recovery in one strategy. It is valuable as a triad-friendly nutrition node: food that serves training capacity while you sleep. Athletes who train hard but under-recover overnight will benefit most. Key training trials often use younger adults, so pair age context with [[D-Nutrition/S11-desbrow-older-athletes|Desbrow]] and sleep quality with [[E-Sleep-and-recovery/S13-walsh-athlete-sleep|Walsh]]. Keep protein dosing grounded in [[D-Nutrition/S28-issn-protein-stand|ISSN]] and doctrine context in [[A-Introduction/S15-fm-7-22-h2f|FM 7-22]]. This is support for capacity, not permission to build a second diet rabbit hole.
