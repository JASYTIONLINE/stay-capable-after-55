## Citation

National Institutes of Health, Office of Dietary Supplements. (n.d.). *Nutrient recommendations and databases*. U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. https://ods.od.nih.gov/HealthInformation/nutrientrecommendations.aspx

**Related:** Vitamin and mineral fact sheets — https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/list-VitaminsMinerals/

## Annotation

NIH ODS publishes Dietary Reference Intake tables (including Recommended Dietary Allowances and Tolerable Upper Intake Levels) and consumer/professional fact sheets for individual vitamins and minerals. It covers what healthy people generally need and where excess intake raises risk — the official deep link for micronutrient personalization that a primer page should not recreate in full. It is valuable as the caution-and-detail hub beside sports-nutrition macros. Athletes after 55 checking vitamin D, calcium, iron, magnesium, zinc, or multivitamin stacking will benefit most. It is a reference library, not a training diet. Pair food-as-fuel planning with [[G-Sources/S34-issn-diets-body-composition|ISSN body composition]] and older-athlete framing with [[G-Sources/S11-strasser-older-athletes|Strasser]]. Supplement regulatory context sits at the [FDA Dietary Supplements page](https://www.fda.gov/food/dietary-supplements).
