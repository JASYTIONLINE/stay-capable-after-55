## Citation

Endo, Y., & Nourmahnad, A. (2020). Optimizing skeletal muscle anabolic response to resistance training in aging. *Frontiers in Physiology, 11*, 874. https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2020.00874

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2020.00874

## Annotation

This peer-reviewed *Frontiers* review explains why the same lifting stimulus often builds less muscle after midlife: aging blunts the anabolic response to resistance training and nutrition compared with younger adults. It covers age-related shifts in muscle protein synthesis, inflammatory and hormonal context, and why progressive loading plus adequate protein matter more—not less—as the body changes. It is valuable because it names a concrete physiological reason the old mid-40s regimen can stall: metabolism and muscle remodeling are not the same game after 50. Athletes who still train hard but see diminishing returns will benefit most. Limitation: much of the clinical framing targets older adults broadly, not only masters or tactical athletes; pair it with [[G-Sources/S04-fragala-nsca-older-adults|Fragala]] for programming and [[G-Sources/S11-strasser-older-athletes|Strasser]] for older-athlete fuel. Use beside [Field Manual (FM) 7-22](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN44522-FM_7-22-002-WEB-7.pdf) · [[G-Sources/S15-fm-7-22-h2f|note]] as evidence that lifestyle demand must adapt to an aging physiology.
