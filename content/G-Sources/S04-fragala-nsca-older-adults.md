## Annotation

This NSCA position statement covers resistance training for older adults, including progressive programs, meaningful intensity, multi-joint lifts, and power work at moderate loads. It covers what “good strength programming after midlife” looks like. It is a credentialed strength and conditioning body resource, not just social media content or marketing material. It is valuable as the professional spine of this section: use it to check hard claims from books, videos, and blogs. Athletes and coaches after 55 who need a baseline grounded in strength and conditioning (S&C) practice will benefit most. The intended audience is broad older adults, so high performers and tactical athletes still individualize for sport and load. Compare still-training research such as [[G-Sources/S01-mckendry-master-athletes|McKendry on master athletes]], then use [[G-Sources/S02-borde-dose-response|Borde]] for dose ranges, [American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM)](https://www.acsm.org) · [[G-Sources/S18-currier-acsm-video|Currier’s video]] for current guideline talk, and [[E-Sleep-and-recovery/index|Sleep and recovery]] when volume outruns sleep.
