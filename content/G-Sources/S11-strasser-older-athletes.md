## Citation

Strasser, B., Pesta, D., Rittweger, J., Burtscher, J., & Burtscher, M. (2021). Nutrition for older athletes: Focus on sex-differences. *Nutrients, 13*(5), 1409. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13051409

**Link:** https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/5/1409

## Annotation

This peer-reviewed *Nutrients* review is written for masters athletes and covers age-related physiology, sex differences, energy and protein needs, bone support, vitamin D, and related micronutrient concerns for people who still train. It covers diet as fuel for capacity, not frail-senior meal advice. Concrete targets discussed include roughly 1.5–1.6 g protein/kg/day with 35–40 g per meal for lean-mass gains under resistance training, higher protein (about 1.6–2.4 g/kg/day) during energy restriction, attention to energy availability for bone, post-exercise carbohydrate, and higher demand for nutrients such as vitamin D. Active athletes after 55 who may be cutting fat while trying to keep muscle will benefit most. It is a broad review rather than a complete meal-by-meal prescription, so pair practical intake structure with [[G-Sources/S29-body-signals-elevate-plate|Elevate Your Plate]], protein standards with [International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN)](https://www.issn.org) · [[G-Sources/S28-issn-protein-stand|note]], and goal-based energy planning with [[G-Sources/S34-issn-diets-body-composition|ISSN diets and body composition]]. Connect overnight strategy to [[G-Sources/S14-trommelen-presleep-protein|Trommelen]], weight-cut evidence to [[G-Sources/S12-verreijen-protein-weight-loss|Verreijen]], calorie-estimate method to [[G-Sources/S35-mifflin-st-jeor|Mifflin–St Jeor]], and micronutrient detail/ULs to [[G-Sources/S36-nih-ods-nutrient-recs|NIH ODS]].
