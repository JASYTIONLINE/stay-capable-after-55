## Doctrine lens (FM 7-22 / Performance Triad)

**Core message used for these comparisons:** Holistic Health and Fitness treats **sleep, activity (physical training), and nutrition as one readiness system** — not three optional hobbies. Capacity under real demand is built and protected by balancing all three; more training alone is not “more ready.” Progressive loading, recovery, and fueling support sustained performance.

**Labels used below**
- **Reinforces** — same core claim in doctrine language or civilian parallel  
- **Agrees (different frame)** — same direction, different audience/method emphasis  
- **Complements** — fills a pillar or skill doctrine assumes but does not teach in depth  
- **Orthogonal** — meta tool for judging sources; not a readiness claim  
- **Tension** — partial conflict or risk of misreading against holistic balance (not necessarily outright contradiction)

No published source here **outright contradicts** FM 7-22’s triad. Tensions are mostly “activity-only” or “single-pillar” emphases that need the rest of the collection to stay honest.

## A. Evidence filter

### S16 — CRAAP Test (Meriam Library)
**Note:** [[A-Evidence-filter/S16-craap-test|Open source note]]  
**Type:** Tool / library guide  
**Cite:** Blakeslee / Meriam Library (CSU Chico). Evaluating information – Applying the CRAAP test. https://library.csuchico.edu/sites/default/files/craap-test.pdf  

**Abstract:** Five-question first-pass screen (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose) for any document or media item. Unique contribution: memorable packaging check before deeper reading. Limitation: a source can pass CRAAP and still be weak in argument — pair with Adler (S17) and still-training examples such as McKendry (S01).  
**FM 7-22:** **Orthogonal.** Does not teach sleep/activity/nutrition; it teaches how to judge claims *about* those pillars (including civilian “H2F” marketing). Reinforces doctrine indirectly by protecting the collection from junk that would dilute readiness logic.

---

### S17 — Adler & Van Doren, *How to Read a Book*
**Note:** [[A-Evidence-filter/S17-adler-how-to-read|Open source note]]  
**Type:** Book  
**Cite:** Adler, M. J., & Van Doren, C. (1972). *How to read a book* (Rev. ed.). Simon & Schuster.

**Abstract:** Critical reading: understand an argument before agreeing or disagreeing; disagree on clear grounds (uninformed, misinformed, illogical, incomplete). Unique contribution: judgment after packaging checks. Limitation: general/aged guide — not a web-misinfo manual; use topic evidence (S01) to test fitness claims.  
**FM 7-22:** **Orthogonal / protective.** Same role as CRAAP at argument depth: keep users from “adopting” training or diet claims that silently contradict holistic readiness (e.g., sleep-optional hustle culture). Complements doctrine by making FM 7-22 itself readable as a system, not a slogan.

### S01 — McKendry et al. (2018)
**Note:** [[A-Evidence-filter/S01-mckendry-master-athletes|Open source note]]  
**Type:** Scholarly (systematic review / meta-analyses)  
**Cite:** McKendry et al. (2018). Muscle morphology and performance in master athletes. *Ageing Research Reviews*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arr.2018.04.007  

**Abstract:** Evidence on chronically trained master athletes’ muscle and performance. On this shelf as a **worked example** of still-training research, not only for findings. Unique contribution: shows what “good fit” evidence looks like after CRAAP/Adler. Limitation: little combat/tactical detail — send that to Athlete practice / Programming.  
**FM 7-22:** **Agrees (different frame).** Reinforces the *activity* pillar’s payoff — long-term training capacity is real in aging bodies — without claiming sleep/nutrition. Slight tension if read as “training alone proves readiness”; doctrine still requires the other pillars. Best used as evidence that H2F’s physical domain is trainable late, not that gym work replaces sleep or fuel.

### S27 — ACSM preparticipation & progressive prep
**Note:** [[B-Returning-to-active-lifestyle/S27-acsm-preparticipation-prep|Open source note]]  
**Type:** Professional / scholarly practice guidance  
**Cite:** Riebe et al. (2015/2016). New preparticipation health screening recommendations. *ACSM’s Health & Fitness Journal*. Related PDF: https://www.acsm.org/docs/default-source/files-for-resource-library/acsmprescreening101.pdf  

**Abstract:** Screening plus progressive transitional phase (~2–3 months), warm-up/cool-down, start low and go slow before vigorous work. Unique contribution: written spine of the comeback shelf. Limitation: professional guidance, not a dramatic video — use S23/S24 for urgency and S31 for cardiac risk of unaccustomed hard effort.  
**FM 7-22:** **Reinforces / agrees (civilian clinical-exercise frame).** Progressive preparation before vigorous intensity is fully aligned with H2F progressive physical training. ACSM is more preparticipation/screening language; FM 7-22 is readiness doctrine — same “earn intensity” idea, different bureaucracy. Explicitly points readiness behaviors (sleep/nutrition) that make the ramp work — triad-compatible.

### S31 — Willich et al. (1993) / *NEJM*
**Note:** [[B-Returning-to-active-lifestyle/S31-willich-exertion-mi-trigger|Open source note]]  
**Type:** Scholarly (observational / trigger study)  
**Cite:** Willich et al. (1993). Physical exertion as a trigger of acute myocardial infarction. *NEJM, 329*(23), 1684–1690. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199312023292302  

**Abstract:** Hard exertion (≥6 METs) linked to temporary MI risk; much higher if you exercise infrequently. Unique contribution: medical evidence behind “too much too soon.” Limitation: early-1990s trigger study, not a training plan or modern screening algorithm.  
**FM 7-22:** **Reinforces (medical warning layer).** Does not teach the triad, but strongly supports why unaccustomed hard activity without a trained base is dangerous — consistent with progressive H2F training and ACSM “start low, go slow.” Regular activity lowers relative risk in the study — agrees with doctrine’s push for habitual physical readiness, not sporadic heroics.

## C. Programming

### S04 — Fragala et al. (2019) / NSCA
**Note:** [[C-Programming/S04-fragala-nsca-older-adults|Open source note]]  
**Type:** Professional position statement  
**Cite:** Fragala et al. (2019). Resistance training for older adults: NSCA position statement. https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000003230  

**Abstract:** Progressive RT, intensity, multijoint work, power at moderate loads for older adults. Unique contribution: professional S&C spine of Programming. Limitation: broad older-adult audience — individualize for high performers / tactical athletes.  
**FM 7-22:** **Complements / agrees (different frame).** Deepens the **activity** pillar with credentialed RT standards H2F assumes must be programmed well. Does not replace sleep/nutrition chapters. Mild **tension** only if treated as the whole of readiness (gym doctrine vs holistic doctrine) — in this collection it is explicitly one pillar.

### S02 — Borde et al. (2015)
**Note:** [[C-Programming/S02-borde-dose-response|Open source note]]  
**Type:** Scholarly (meta-analysis)  
**Cite:** Borde, Hortobágyi, & Granacher (2015). Dose–response relationships of resistance training in healthy old adults.  

**Abstract:** How often, how hard, how much older adults should lift (dose–response). Unique contribution: quantitative dose layer beside Fragala. Limitation: healthy old adults — transfer carefully to still-competing masters.  
**FM 7-22:** **Complements.** Gives evidence-based dose for physical training — compatible with progressive H2F programming. **Tension if misused:** chasing “optimal dose” while ignoring recovery/sleep/fuel can contradict triad balance; Sullivan (S07) and Walsh (S13) are the corrective.

### S03 — Markov et al. (2023)

**Type:** Scholarly (review / meta-analysis)

**Abstract:** Mixing lifting with endurance (run, ruck, conditioning). Unique contribution: concurrent-training shelf piece for martial/tactical athletes. Limitation: review-level — still needs individual recovery honesty (D shelf).  
**FM 7-22:** **Reinforces / agrees.** Military readiness rarely is “strength only” or “cardio only”; concurrent work mirrors multi-domain physical demand. Agrees with H2F’s broad physical readiness while reminding that stacking modalities raises recovery demand — triad still required.
