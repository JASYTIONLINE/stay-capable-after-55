## How to use this page

For each source, read in this order:

1. **What it actually is** — content, not wishful framing  
2. **Credibility** — authority, venue, currency, purpose/bias, accuracy signals  
3. **Topic fit** — shelf (A–E), quota tags, still-training vs sedentary senior  
4. **Overlap** — what else covers the same slot  
5. **Include if… / Cut if…** — decision tests  
6. **Draft verdict** — my suggested starting call; override freely  
7. Mark your final: `KEEP` / `CUT` / `MAYBE` and a one-line reason

## A. Evidence filter

### 01 — McKendry et al., 2018 (master athletes systematic review)

**Citation short:** McKendry et al. (2018). Muscle morphology and performance in master athletes: A systematic review and meta-analyses. *Ageing Research Reviews*.  
**Links:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29715523/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arr.2018.04.007)  
**Type:** Peer-reviewed systematic review / meta-analyses · **Shelf:** A · **Bib:** S01

**What the source actually covers**  
Synthesizes evidence on chronically trained master athletes: muscle morphology (size/architecture) and performance outcomes across training modes. Core claim: long-term training changes aging muscle and performance relative to untrained older adults. Useful as proof that “older athlete” is a different population than “older sedentary.”

**Credibility**  
- **Authority:** Peer-reviewed in a major aging journal; multi-author academic team.  
- **Accuracy:** Systematic review + meta-analyses (high method tier vs single study or blog).  
- **Currency:** 2018 — still current for this topic; not obsolete.  
- **Purpose:** Scientific synthesis, not product sales.  
- **Relevance:** High for Evidence filter; directly matches still-training masters.

**Topic fit / quotas**  
Anchor for “research on people who kept training.” Supports rejecting generic senior-fitness advice.

**Overlap**  
Overlaps Aagaard (06) as primary trail; overlaps ACSM/Food & Fitness (03) at practitioner translation level. McKendry is the stronger scholarly shelf piece.
