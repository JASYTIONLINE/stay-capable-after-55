## Annotation

This NSCA position statement covers resistance training for older adults, including progressive programs, meaningful intensity, multijoint lifts, and power work at moderate loads. It covers what “good strength programming after midlife” looks like from a credentialed strength and conditioning body, not from internet templates. It is valuable as the professional spine of this shelf: use it to check hard claims from books, videos, and blogs. Athletes and coaches after 55 who need a baseline grounded in S&C practice will benefit most. The intended audience is broad older adults, so high performers and tactical athletes still individualize for sport and load. Screen unfamiliar advice with the [[A-Evidence-filter/S16-craap-test|CRAAP test]] and [[A-Evidence-filter/S17-adler-how-to-read|Adler’s framework]], compare still-training research such as [[A-Evidence-filter/S01-mckendry-master-athletes|McKendry on master athletes]], then use [[C-Programming/S02-borde-dose-response|Borde]] for dose ranges, [[C-Programming/S18-currier-acsm-video|Currier’s ACSM video]] for current guideline talk, and [[D-Load-and-recovery/index|Load and recovery]] when volume outruns sleep.
