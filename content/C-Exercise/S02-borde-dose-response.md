# S02 — Borde et al. (2015)

**Category:** [[C-Exercise/index|C — Exercise]]
**Type:** Scholarly

## Citation

Borde, R., Hortobágyi, T., & Granacher, U. (2015). Dose–response relationships of resistance training in healthy old adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Sports Medicine, 45*(12), 1693–1720. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-015-0385-9

**Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4656698/

## Annotation

This meta-analysis examines how resistance-training frequency, intensity, volume, and related variables affect strength outcomes in healthy older adults. It covers dose–response evidence you can use to design or audit a plan after 55 instead of guessing from social media templates. It is valuable as a programming map: how often, how hard, and how much, grounded in pooled studies. Athletes and coaches who need evidence-based ranges will benefit most. The sample is “healthy old adults,” not necessarily competitive masters or tactical athletes, so triangulate hard prescriptions with [[C-Exercise/S04-fragala-nsca-older-adults|Fragala’s NSCA statement]] and individual recovery capacity under [[E-Sleep-and-recovery/index|Sleep and recovery]]. Pair with [[C-Exercise/S03-markov-concurrent|Markov]] when lifting shares the week with endurance, and with [[A-Triad-and-judgment/S01-mckendry-master-athletes|McKendry]] when you need reminder that still-training bodies are not the same population as sedentary seniors. If a new program fails [[A-Triad-and-judgment/S16-craap-test|CRAAP]] or Adler’s logic checks, do not let its volume numbers outrank this evidence.
