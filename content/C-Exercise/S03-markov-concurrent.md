# S03 — Markov et al. (2023)

**Category:** [[C-Exercise/index|C — Exercise]]
**Type:** Scholarly

## Citation

Markov, A., Hauser, L., & Chaabene, H. (2023). Effects of concurrent strength and endurance training on measures of physical fitness in healthy middle-aged and older adults: A systematic review with meta-analysis. *Sports Medicine, 53*, 437–455. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01764-2

**Link:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-022-01764-2

## Annotation

This review covers concurrent strength and endurance training in adults roughly 50–73 and how session design and sequencing affect strength, power, and conditioning outcomes. It covers the dual-mode problem athletes know well: lift and also run, ruck, or train martial arts without burying strength under junk conditioning volume. It is valuable for occupational and sport athletes after 55 who live under mixed demand, not gym-only hypertrophy plans. Athletes and coaches managing interference will benefit most. It offers principles more than a single weekly template, so individual recovery still rules — especially sleep under [[E-Sleep-and-recovery/index|Sleep and recovery]]. Use it beside [[C-Exercise/S04-fragala-nsca-older-adults|Fragala]] for the strength spine and [[C-Exercise/S02-borde-dose-response|Borde]] for dose math. When a concurrent program looks clever but thin on authority, run [[A-Triad-and-judgment/S16-craap-test|CRAAP]] and [[A-Triad-and-judgment/S17-adler-how-to-read|Adler]] before you rearrange your week around it.
