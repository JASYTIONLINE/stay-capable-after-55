---

## Section 1 - Why the old plan stops working (A)

- Welcome the reader
- Personal story: knee injury from returning to running too hard, too soon; Guard and martial arts stakes
- Growing old is not an excuse; lifestyle still requires climbing
- Who this is for: still-training athletes after midlife (and coaches), not sedentary senior fitness, not a diet library
- What they already know vs what they need now
- Promise: clear reason the mid-40s playbook stalls; a few high-leverage changes; one readiness map
- Performance Triad: sleep, activity, nutrition as one system (FM 7-22 / H2F frame)
- Physiology aha: same stimulus can buy less muscle after midlife (anabolic response)
- Bone and strength still trainable with progressive loading
- Still-training bodies are not sedentary older-adult samples
- Bridge: if coming back from time off, do not chase the old self yet

---

## Section 2 - Come back without getting hurt (B)

- Problem: mind remembers old intensity; tissue does not
- Clearance is not readiness
- Progressive prep: start low, go slow, warm up, earn intensity
- Too much too soon / ego volume (tie to personal knee story)
- Unaccustomed hard effort and cardiac risk for infrequent exercisers
- Practical steps: clearance when needed; weeks of base; warm-up; volume honesty
- Lived comeback under real demand (long-game rebuild)
- Bridge: when the base is honest, train hard enough on purpose

---

## Section 3 - Train hard enough on purpose (C)

- Activity pillar for people under real demand
- Progressive resistance still works: intensity, multi-joint work, power
- Dose matters: frequency, intensity, volume
- Strength and bone stay trainable
- Tie to earlier physiology: smarter stimulus, not softer by default
- Concurrent work (lift + run/ruck/martial arts) must be designed
- Lived practice fills gaps science rarely speaks to
- Still-training research as a better mirror than sedentary samples
- Bridge: fuel the work

---

## Section 4 - Fuel for the work (D)

- Nutrition as capacity support, not a second diet hobby
- Protein and timing serve the training
- Older-athlete needs vs young-athlete templates
- Cutting without throwing away muscle (protein + lifting)
- Overnight / sleep-window fueling as triad thinking
- Practical intake structure without gadget worship
- Bridge: protect the sessions you just fueled

---

## Section 5 - Protect the work (E)

- Sleep as capacity, not optional wellness
- Under-sleep erases hard weeks
- Masters volume sensitivity: just add more often fails
- Recovery habits as part of the job
- Tie physiology, comeback risk, and volume honesty together
- Bridge into the close

---

## Section 6 - Mid-40s vs after 55 (F)

- Synthesis close, not a new topic
- One claim: adapt the lifestyle to the aging body without abandoning a demanding life
- How sections 1-5 fit together in one sentence each
- Keep-this list:
  1. Recovery windows shrink
  2. Same stimulus can buy less muscle
  3. Bone and strength still trainable
  4. Clearance is not readiness; ego volume gets people hurt
  5. Volume honesty beats just add more
  6. Still-training bodies are not stopped-senior bodies
  7. One readiness system (sleep, activity, nutrition)
- Leave with a few high-leverage changes, not a new fitness religion
