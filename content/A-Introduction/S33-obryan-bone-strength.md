## Citation

O’Bryan, S. J., Giuliano, C., Woessner, M. N., Vogrin, S., Smith, C., Duque, G., & Levinger, I. (2022). Progressive resistance training for concomitant increases in muscle strength and bone mineral density in older adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Sports Medicine, 52*, 1939–1959. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01675-2

**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01675-2

## Annotation

This *Sports Medicine* systematic review and meta-analysis asks whether progressive resistance training can slow age-related loss of both muscle strength and bone mineral density. It covers pooled trial evidence that training increases lower-limb strength and femur/hip bone density in older adults, with clearer certainty for strength than for every bone site. It is valuable because it ties two midlife fears—getting weaker and getting more brittle—to a single adaptation lever still available to people who train with purpose. Active adults after 55 who need evidence that hard training protects tissue, not just vanity metrics, will benefit most. Limitation: included trials often enroll adults 65+, so transfer carefully to mid-50s tactical or martial athletes; programming detail still belongs with [[C-Exercise/S04-fragala-nsca-older-adults|Fragala]] and [[C-Exercise/S02-borde-dose-response|Borde]]. Pair with [[A-Introduction/S32-endo-anabolic-aging|Endo]] on anabolic resistance to show both the problem (blunted response) and a bone-and-strength reason to keep progressive loading in the plan.
