# S17 — How to Read a Book (Adler)

**Category:** [[A-Evidence-filter/index|A — Evidence filter]]  
**Type:** Book

## Citation

Adler, M. J., & Van Doren, C. (1972). *How to read a book: The classic guide to intelligent reading* (Rev. ed.). Simon & Schuster. (See especially the chapters on analytical reading and agreeing or disagreeing with an author.)

## Annotation

This classic guide teaches how to understand an author’s argument before you accept or reject it, then how to disagree on clear grounds: the author may be uninformed, misinformed, illogical, or incomplete. It covers disciplined reading for judgment, not speed-skimming for quotes. It is valuable because [[A-Evidence-filter/S16-craap-test|CRAAP-style checklists]] catch packaging problems; Adler catches thinking problems — including the habit of dismissing a hard source before you can fairly restate what it says. Readers who want to evaluate studies, books, doctrine, and popular advice with more than “I liked it” or “I don’t trust it” will benefit most. Its limitation is age and generality: it is not a modern web-misinformation manual, and it will not tell you whether a fitness study sampled sedentary seniors or master athletes. Pair it with the [[A-Evidence-filter/S16-craap-test|CRAAP test]] for a fast credibility screen, then use topic-specific evidence such as [[A-Evidence-filter/S01-mckendry-master-athletes|McKendry on chronically trained masters]] to judge whether a fitness claim fits someone who still trains after 55.
