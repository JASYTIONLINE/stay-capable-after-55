# S16 — CRAAP Test

**Category:** [[A-Evidence-filter/index|A — Evidence filter]]

## Citation

Blakeslee, S. / Meriam Library, California State University, Chico. (n.d.). *Evaluating information – Applying the CRAAP test*. https://library.csuchico.edu/sites/default/files/craap-test.pdf

## Annotation

This short library handout walks through five questions for any source you meet: Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, and Purpose. It covers a practical first pass before you invest time in a full read — Is this current enough for your question? Does it actually fit what you need? Who produced it, and why (to inform, teach, sell, or persuade)? It is valuable because it gives a memorable, repeatable method for sorting information instead of relying on gut feel or a familiar logo. Anyone building or using a curated collection, and anyone drowning in search results on any topic, will find it useful. Its limitation is that CRAAP is a surface screen: a source can look fine on the checklist and still be weak in argument, incomplete for your situation, or wrong in the details. Use it first, then go deeper with [[A-Evidence-filter/S17-adler-how-to-read|Adler’s critical-reading framework]] and, for this site’s topic, compare what you found against research on people who actually kept training, such as [[A-Evidence-filter/S01-mckendry-master-athletes|McKendry et al. on master athletes]].
