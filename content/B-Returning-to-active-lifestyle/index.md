# B. Returning - Come back without getting hurt

You already know the old intensity. My working assumption, from experience: the danger is pretending your tissue remembers it as well as your mind does. After [[A-Introduction/index|Introduction]] explains why I say the mid-40s plan can stall, this section is about not destroying the comeback while you fix it.

## Clearance is not readiness

My synthesis of coaching and prep guidance: a clinician clearing you to move is not the same as being prepared for sport-level stress, fight pace, or old Physical Training (PT) standards. Pain improving or a “you are fine” note still leaves you short on strength, impact tolerance, and progressive loading. That gap, in my view, reinjures people who rush. Progressive preparation guidance from the [American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM)](https://www.acsm.org)<sup>[[G-Sources/S27-acsm-preparticipation-prep|S27]]</sup> ([journal link](https://journals.lww.com/acsm-healthfitness/fulltext/2016/05000/new_preparticipation_health_screening.9.aspx)) covers screening when needed, then earning intensity with a transitional ramp, warm-up, and “start low and go slow.”

## Too much too soon

The failure mode I know best is ego volume: old mileage, old session length, old terrain, because memory outruns capacity. That is the story behind my knee, and it is why I argue hard programming in [[C-Exercise/index|Exercise]] has to wait for an honest base. Popular coaching media names the same trap<sup>[[G-Sources/S24-video-too-much-too-soon|S24]]</sup> · <sup>[[G-Sources/S23-video-cleared-not-ready|S23]]</sup>. Lock the principle with ACSM prep<sup>[[G-Sources/S27-acsm-preparticipation-prep|S27]]</sup>, not a numbered plan from social media.

## Unaccustomed hard effort is not only an orthopedic problem

A classic *New England Journal of Medicine* study found that strenuous exertion can trigger myocardial infarction (MI), with much higher short-term risk among people who exercise infrequently<sup>[[G-Sources/S31-willich-exertion-mi-trigger|S31]]</sup> ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199312023292302)). That does not replace medical care. My conclusion from that evidence: “I used to be able to” is not a safety plan. [[E-Sleep-and-recovery/index|Sleep and recovery]] and [[D-Nutrition/index|Nutrition]] matter here too. My triad rule again: readiness is a system, not a tough week.

## What to do

1. Get appropriate clearance when the situation calls for it.  
2. Spend weeks building a base before you chase old personal records.  
3. Warm up for vigorous work. Progress duration and intensity on purpose.  
4. When ego whispers the old self, answer with the volume honesty in [[E-Sleep-and-recovery/index|Sleep and recovery]].

Lived comeback stories under real demand help too. The Mash comeback conversation<sup>[[G-Sources/S26-podcast-mash-comeback|S26]]</sup> is a long-game mirror of rebuilding after time off.

When the base is honest, [[C-Exercise/index|Exercise]] is where you train hard enough on purpose.
