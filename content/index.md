Curator: 1SG John Barkle

## Introduction

Welcome to the rabbit hole. Most fitness-after-midlife advice is written for the wrong person. Born in 1970, I am in my mid-fifties, an assistant instructor in Kuk Sool Won, and an actively drilling First Sergeant in the Texas National Guard. I still train under real demand on the mat and in uniform.

Growing old is not an excuse for being out of shape. My body may be over the hill, but my lifestyle requires me to keep climbing. Too many publications treat older adults as sedentary and chase diet or exercise alone when capacity needs a holistic approach. I lived that broken pattern too long: injuries, sleep deprivation, and nutritional deficits while cutting weight. I built this collection for myself and others who want to stay fit later — including people returning after time off who need to prepare first, then train hard.

This collection is for adults 55 and older who train with purpose: martial arts, tactical or occupational fitness (police, fire, military), and serious recreational athletics. It is also for coaches sorting conflicting social and marketing advice. It is not a weight-loss library or a “just stay active” list.

The organizing idea is Army doctrine: the Performance Triad of sleep, activity, and nutrition inside Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) in FM 7-22 — one readiness system, not three hobbies. Sources expand that idea with civilian research, professional guidance, and teachers whose lives match their practice.
