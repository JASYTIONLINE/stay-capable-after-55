# Stay Capable After 55

**A curated collection on strength and conditioning (S&C) for athletes who remain active after 55**

Curator: First Sergeant (1SG) John Barkle

## Introduction

Welcome to the rabbit hole. Most fitness-after-midlife advice is written not written for me. Born in 1970, I am in my mid-fifties, an assistant instructor in the traditional Martial Arts System Kuk Sool Won, and an actively drilling First Sergeant in the Texas National Guard. I still train under real demand on the mat and in uniform.

Growing old is not an excuse for being out of shape. My body may be over the hill, but my lifestyle requires me to keep climbing. Too many publications treat older adults as sedentary and chase diet or exercise alone when capacity needs a holistic approach. I have been  living that broken pattern too long. I have experienced multiple issues like injuries, sleep deprivation, and nutritional deficits while cutting weight. I built this collection for myself and others who want to stay fit later in life, especially those returning after time off  who need to prepare first, then train hard.  As we age the amount of time it takes for atrophy to set in decreases from weeks to days. Another area most common fitness publications miss is recovery time.  For younger athletes, recovery from injury could be measured in days or weeks, whereas for older individuals that time could stretch out months and even years.

This collection is for adults 55 and older who train with purpose: martial artists, tactical or occupational fitness (police, fire, military), and serious recreational athletics. It is also for coaches sorting conflicting social and marketing advice. It is not a weight-loss library or a “just stay active” list.

The core concept behind this program is: the Performance Triad of sleep, activity, and nutrition inside [Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F)](https://www.army.mil/article/239475/holistic_health_added_to_army_fitness_doctrine) in [Field Manual (FM) 7-22](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN44522-FM_7-22-002-WEB-7.pdf) — one readiness system, not three unrelated disciplines. Sources expand that idea with civilian research, professional guidance, and teachers whose lives match their practice.

The sections below move from framework to practice: judge sources, return safely, train, fuel, and recover. Each opens with annotated sources — click a title for the full write-up.
