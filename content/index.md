Curator: 1SG John Barkle

## Introduction

Welcome to the rabbit hole. Most fitness-after-midlife advice is written for the wrong person. Born in 1970, I am in my mid-fifties, an assistant instructor in Kuk Sool Won, and an actively drilling First Sergeant in the Texas National Guard. I still train under real demand on the mat and in uniform.

Growing old is not an excuse for being out of shape. My body may be over the hill, but my lifestyle requires me to keep climbing. Too many publications treat older adults as sedentary and chase diet or exercise alone when capacity needs a holistic approach. I lived that broken pattern too long: injuries, sleep deprivation, and nutritional deficits while cutting weight. I built this for myself and others who want to stay fit later. That includes people returning after time off — prepare first, then train hard.

This collection is for adults 55 and older who train with purpose: martial arts, tactical or occupational fitness (like police and fire), and recreational athletics. It is also for coaches who need a clear path through conflicting social and marketing advice. It is for those with an active lifestyle, not for losing a few pounds or "just staying active."

The core of this collection stems from Army doctrine: the Performance Triad of sleep, activity, and nutrition, now part of Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) in FM 7-22. This doctrine combines all three into one readiness system. This site expands its research beyond the force to civilian medical and professional guidance, athletes who still train after midlife, and occupational fitness under real demand. Sources include teaching from individuals whose lifestyles embody their teaching. Excluded as primary focus: clinical rehab, influencer routines without authority, and diet-first collections.
